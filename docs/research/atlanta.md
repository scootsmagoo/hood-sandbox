# Atlanta research: districts, landmarks, transport, police, culture, naming

*Research pass, 14 October 2026. **[unsure]** means the fact could not be confirmed. **[2026]** means it was checked against recent news.*

## 1. Districts

### Intown Atlanta (City of Atlanta)

- **Downtown**
  - Towers, convention hotels, the tourist core and government: the Gold Dome Capitol, City Hall and the courts.
  - Busy on event days and dead at night.
  - Streets: Peachtree St, Marietta St, MLK Jr Dr, Auburn Ave (Sweet Auburn).
- **Midtown**
  - Dense high-rises, the arts district, Georgia Tech next door.
  - Young professionals, LGBTQ+ nightlife, the Atlanta Fed.
  - Streets: Peachtree, 10th, 14th, West Peachtree.
- **Buckhead**
  - Old money and new towers. The West Paces Ferry mansions, Lenox and Phipps malls, the bar district.
  - Streets: Peachtree Rd, Piedmont Rd, Roswell Rd.
- **Old Fourth Ward**
  - MLK's birthplace. BeltLine lofts (Ponce City Market) next to historic Black blocks.
  - Streets: Edgewood, Boulevard, Ponce.
- **Inman Park / Poncey-Highland**
  - Victorian homes, restaurants, BeltLine crowds. The Clermont Lounge.
- **Little Five Points**
  - Bohemian: vintage shops, punk, tattoo parlors, the Variety Playhouse.
  - Streets: Euclid Ave, Moreland Ave.
- **Cabbagetown / Reynoldstown / Grant Park / Summerhill**
  - Mill cottages, Krog Street Tunnel graffiti, Oakland Cemetery, Zoo Atlanta, and the old Turner Field (now Georgia State's stadium).
- **East Atlanta Village**
  - Grungy bars and music, mixed income. "Zone 6" is a trap-music reference point.
- **Westside / West Midtown**
  - Warehouses turned breweries, design shops and lofts.
  - Howell Mill, Marietta St, Westside Park (an old quarry).
- **Bankhead / Grove Park / English Ave / Vine City**
  - Poor, historically Black, under development pressure next to the stadium.
  - Hollowell Pkwy is the old Bankhead Hwy.
- **West End / AUC**
  - The historically Black colleges of the Atlanta University Center (Morehouse, Spelman, Clark Atlanta), Black-owned business, the BeltLine Westside Trail.
  - Ralph David Abernathy Blvd.
- **Southwest Atlanta (SWATS) / Cascade**
  - Black middle-class suburbia inside the city.
- **Castleberry Hill**
  - A gallery and loft pocket next to the stadium.
- **Atlantic Station**
  - Planned mixed-use development on an old steel-mill site.
- **Decatur**
  - Its own city, in DeKalb County. A progressive college-town square with a strong food scene.

### North Fulton

- **Sandy Springs**: corporate towers along GA-400 and I-285 (the "King and Queen" towers, the UPS and Mercedes-Benz USA headquarters) and apartments along Roswell Rd.
- **Roswell**: a historic mill town, with Canton St nightlife and the Chattahoochee riverfront.
- **Alpharetta**: a wealthy tech suburb: Avalon, the Big Creek Greenway, the amphitheatre.
- **Johns Creek / Milton**: affluent cul-de-sacs; Milton is horse country.

### North DeKalb

- **Brookhaven**: young-professional apartments, Town Brookhaven, the southern stretch of Buford Hwy.
- **Chamblee**: Asian and Latino strip malls, antique row, PDK airport.
- **Doraville**: the heart of Buford Highway. Assembly Atlanta studios stand on the old GM plant. Historically a speed trap.
- **Dunwoody / Perimeter**: Perimeter Center office parks, Perimeter Mall, State Farm's campus.
- **Tucker**: a blue-collar Main Street. It has been a city since 2016 but has no police department of its own; DeKalb County police cover it.

### Cobb County

- **Marietta**: the county seat. Marietta Square, the Big Chicken, Dobbins Air Reserve Base, the Lockheed plant.
- **Smyrna**: suburban, with a revitalized Market Village; close to The Battery.
- **Vinings / Cumberland**: the Home Depot headquarters, Cumberland Mall, the Galleria, Truist Park and The Battery at I-75/I-285.
- **Kennesaw**: KSU, Town Center, Kennesaw Mountain, the Southern Museum (home of the "General" locomotive).
- **Acworth**: Lake Acworth and Lake Allatoona.
- **Austell**: working class; Six Flags Over Georgia.
- **Mableton**: a city since 2023, now forming its own police department **[2026]**.
- **Powder Springs**: exurban, on the Silver Comet Trail.

### Gwinnett County (one of the most diverse counties in the US)

- **Norcross**: a historic depot downtown. Jimmy Carter Blvd is a Latino and immigrant commercial strip.
- **Peachtree Corners**: office parks and the Curiosity Lab self-driving test road. No city police; Gwinnett County police cover it.
- **Duluth**: Koreatown on Pleasant Hill Rd, and the Gas South District (arena).
- **Lawrenceville**: the county seat: the courthouse square, the jail, Gwinnett Field, Briscoe Field airport.
- **Lilburn**: a modest suburb, home to the BAPS Hindu mandir.
- **Snellville**: big-box sprawl along US-78.
- **Suwanee**: a family suburb with Town Center Park.
- **Buford**: the Mall of Georgia and the road to Lake Lanier.

## 2. Landmarks and points of interest

- **Downtown**
  - Mercedes-Benz Stadium (it hosted eight 2026 World Cup matches **[2026]**), State Farm Arena.
  - Centennial Olympic Park, Georgia Aquarium, World of Coca-Cola, the College Football Hall of Fame.
  - The Capitol, Underground Atlanta, Five Points, Centennial Yards (the old Gulch), the MLK National Historical Park.
- **Midtown**: the Fox Theatre, Piedmont Park, Atlanta Botanical Garden, the High Museum, Georgia Tech and Bobby Dodd Stadium, The Varsity, the Federal Reserve Bank (heist bait).
- **Ponce and Old Fourth Ward**: Ponce City Market (the old Sears building), the BeltLine Eastside Trail.
- **Buckhead**: Lenox Square, Phipps Plaza, the Governor's Mansion.
- **Southside**: Grady Memorial (the trauma center), Fulton County Jail on Rice St, Tyler Perry Studios at Fort McPherson, USP Atlanta.
- **East**: the CDC on Clifton Rd, Emory, East Lake Golf Club, and the Atlanta Public Safety Training Center ("Cop City"; opened April 2025 in DeKalb; politically charged).
- **Cobb**: Truist Park and The Battery, the Big Chicken, Marietta Square, Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park, Six Flags Over Georgia and White Water, Lake Allatoona, the Silver Comet Trail.
- **North**: the Chattahoochee River National Recreation Area ("shoot the Hooch" tubing), Avalon, Lake Lanier (houseboats, drowning folklore, the flooded town of Oscarville, which is sensitive history).
- **Gwinnett**: Gas South Arena (Gladiators hockey), the Mall of Georgia, Gwinnett Field (renamed from Coolray Field in 2026 **[2026]**).
- **Stone Mountain**: the granite dome with the Confederate carving and a KKK history. Handle with care.
- **Airports**
  - Hartsfield-Jackson, the world's busiest by passengers in most years. It is south of the planned map.
  - PDK in Chamblee.
  - Fulton County–Brown Field.
  - McCollum Field in Kennesaw.
  - Briscoe Field in Lawrenceville.
  - Dobbins Air Reserve Base.
- **Atlanta Motor Speedway**: in Hampton, about 30 miles south, so off the map. It could be a fast-travel point or an edge event.

## 3. Transport

### Interstates and freeways

- **I-285, "the Perimeter"**: a 64-mile loop. The "Top End" across the north is where the jams are.
- **I-75**: runs northwest through Cobb.
- **I-85**: runs northeast through Gwinnett.
- **I-20**: runs east–west across the south.
- **GA-400**: from Buckhead north to Alpharetta.
- **I-575**: from I-75 at Kennesaw north to Canton.
- **I-985**: from I-85 at Suwanee toward Lanier.
- **US-78** (the Stone Mountain Freeway) and **GA-316** (toward Athens).

### Arterials

- The many Peachtrees: Peachtree St, Peachtree Rd, Peachtree Industrial Blvd. The number of streets named Peachtree is a running joke.
- Ponce de Leon Ave, US-41 (Cobb Pkwy), Buford Hwy (GA-13), Roswell Rd, Memorial Dr, Moreland Ave, Northside Dr, Fulton Industrial Blvd, Pleasant Hill Rd, Satellite Blvd, Holcomb Bridge Rd.

### Express lanes

These are Peach Pass toll lanes, priced by demand. Most have barriers and few entries and exits, which makes them good chase set-pieces.

- **I-85 Express**.
- **Northwest Corridor** on I-75 and I-575 (reversible).
- **I-75 South Metro** (reversible).
- **GA-400**: express lanes under construction since spring 2026, due around 2031 **[2026]**.
- **I-285 Top End**: federally approved in Oct 2025; construction start not confirmed **[2026]**.

### MARTA

- **Rail lines**, all meeting at **Five Points**:
  - Red: North Springs to the Airport.
  - Gold: Doraville to the Airport.
  - Blue: H.E. Holmes to Indian Creek.
  - Green: Bankhead to Edgewood/Candler Park.
- **Five Points renovation**: started around 2025; current status **[unsure]**.
- **Atlanta Streetcar**: a Downtown loop.
- **BeltLine rail**:
  - The streetcar's eastern extension was halted in Jan 2026.
  - In Sept 2026 the city council backed a 22-mile, $3.5B BeltLine light-rail plan **[2026]**.
- **Summerhill bus rapid transit**: under construction in early 2026; whether it has opened is **[unsure]**.

### Other transit

- Buses: CobbLinc, Ride Gwinnett, and ATL Xpress commuter coaches.
- Amtrak's Crescent stops at the small Peachtree station in Brookwood.

## 4. Law enforcement and emergency services

### Agencies

- **City police: Atlanta Police Department (APD)**
  - Six patrol zones that map neatly onto game districts:
    - Zone 1: Westside and Bankhead
    - Zone 2: Buckhead
    - Zone 3: Southside
    - Zone 4: Southwest
    - Zone 5: Downtown and Midtown
    - Zone 6: East Atlanta
  - The city has a dense camera network, linked to a Real-Time Crime Center.
- **County police**
  - DeKalb, Cobb and Gwinnett each have a county police department. They cover unincorporated areas, plus Tucker, Peachtree Corners and Mableton until Mableton's own department starts (planned for 2027 **[2026]**).
  - Fulton: mostly a sheriff, with a small county police force **[unsure]**.
- **Sheriffs**: one per county. They run the jails and courts.
- **City police departments**: Marietta, Smyrna, Kennesaw, Acworth, Austell, Powder Springs, Sandy Springs, Roswell, Alpharetta, Johns Creek, Milton, Brookhaven, Dunwoody, Chamblee, Doraville, Norcross, Duluth, Lawrenceville, Lilburn, Snellville, Suwanee and Buford.
- **State**
  - Georgia State Patrol (GSP), which patrols the interstates. Its crime-suppression unit has been sent into Atlanta against street racing (the unit's current name is **[unsure]**).
  - The Georgia Bureau of Investigation (GBI), headquartered in DeKalb.
  - Georgia Capitol Police.
- **Other**
  - MARTA Police.
  - Campus police at Georgia Tech, Georgia State and Emory.
  - GDOT's HERO trucks (highway incident responders).
  - The FBI Atlanta field office.
- **Fire**: Atlanta Fire Rescue, plus the county fire departments (Cobb, DeKalb, Gwinnett, Sandy Springs and others).
- **EMS and hospitals**
  - Grady EMS covers Atlanta and much of Fulton (exact area **[unsure]**).
  - Grady is the Level I trauma center. Wellstar Kennestone and Northside Gwinnett are major trauma hospitals (their trauma levels are **[unsure]**).
  - Atlanta Medical Center closed in 2022.

### Pursuit policies: the strongest jurisdiction mechanic

- **APD (restrictive)**
  - A no-chase policy took effect in Jan 2020.
  - Since late 2020, pursuit is allowed only for forcible felonies posing imminent danger. A supervisor must approve, and at most 3 cars may join.
  - No chases for property crimes, traffic offenses or misdemeanors.
  - Whether it has been revised since is **[unsure]**.
- **GSP (aggressive)**
  - Policy 17.02 allows PIT maneuvers when troopers judge them necessary, with no speed cap, and an intentional PIT is not reviewed as a crash.
  - An AJC investigation tied GSP PITs to 19 deaths over five years.
  - After a fatal chase in Little Five Points in April 2025, the Atlanta city council passed a symbolic resolution. No policy change has been confirmed.
- **Translation into the game**
  - **Minor crimes on city streets:** police give up the vehicle chase, respond on foot, and lean on the cameras. Heat builds as a slower "investigation".
  - **Interstates and express lanes:** troopers PIT at speed, and heat escalates fast.
  - **County and suburban departments:** in between (their real policies weren't researched).
  - **Small-city departments:** ticket-happy, like Doraville's speed trap.
  - **Transit stations:** MARTA police control them.
  - **Top heat:** GBI and FBI task forces.
- **Street racing law**
  - In 2024 Georgia raised penalties for street racing and takeovers (SB 10): escalating fines, and the car seized after a second conviction. Whether it passed as written is **[unsure]**.
  - Atlanta and Sandy Springs have impound ordinances.
  - In the game, a repeat racing bust means the car gets impounded.

## 5. Culture and side activities

### Culture

- **Music**
  - OutKast and the Dungeon Family; the So So Def and LaFace labels.
  - Trap: T.I., Jeezy and Gucci Mane, then Future, Migos, Young Thug, 21 Savage and Lil Baby.
  - "Zone 6" and "Bankhead" come up again and again in the lyrics.
  - Strip clubs are famous as places where DJs break new records.
  - **Avoid real gangs and the RICO cases.**
- **Food**
  - Waffle House: founded in Avondale Estates, headquartered in Norcross, and the inspiration for FEMA's informal "Waffle House Index".
  - Lemon-pepper wet wings.
  - The Varsity.
  - Chick-fil-A, founded in Hapeville.
  - Buford Highway's Korean, Vietnamese, Chinese and Mexican restaurants.
  - Locals' nicknames for grocery stores ("Murder Kroger", "Disco Kroger").
- **Freaknik**: an HBCU spring-break gathering from the 1980s to about 2000 that ended in citywide gridlock. A Hulu documentary came out in 2024.
- **Car culture**: donks, box Chevys and candy paint; street takeovers (donuts at intersections and on I-285). Slabs and swangas are Houston culture, not Atlanta, so don't mix them up.
- **Sport**: the Braves, Falcons, United, Hawks, Dream, Gladiators, Stripers and Georgia Tech.
- **Film ("Y'allywood")**: the state film tax credit, Tyler Perry Studios, Trilith, Assembly Atlanta.
- **Environment**
  - Traffic: the Top End, the Connector, the 2017 collapse of the I-85 bridge after a fire, the 2014 "Snowpocalypse".
  - Weather and seasons: yellow pollen (Mar–Apr), summer storms and flooding (Helene, 2024), kudzu.
- **Events**: Dragon Con (Labor Day weekend), the Peachtree Road Race (July 4), the Stone Mountain laser show, the Tour Championship at East Lake.

## 6. Legal and IP naming

### Generally safe to keep

- **Geography:** street, neighborhood, city and county names, rivers, mountains, lakes and the layout.
- **Building appearances:** 17 USC §120(a) allows pictures of buildings visible from public places. How far that extends to 3D game models is untested.
- **Realistic depictions of products:** courts have protected them in games (*AM General v. Activision*, 2020, over Humvees in Call of Duty).
- **The limit:** *Jack Daniel's v. VIP* (2023) narrowed that protection when a mark is used as a source identifier. Using a parody brand as our own logo or merch is riskier.

### Rename or parody

- **Sponsor names on venues:** Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Truist Park, State Farm Arena, Gas South Arena, the Ameris Bank Amphitheatre.
- **Brands:** Coca-Cola, Delta, Home Depot, Chick-fil-A, Waffle House (its yellow-tile sign is trade dress), KFC (keep the Big Chicken itself, change the restaurant), UPS, CNN, Publix, Kroger, The Varsity.
- **Attractions:** Six Flags, the Georgia Aquarium, the Fox Theatre name, Ponce City Market, The Battery, Lenox, the Mall of Georgia, Avalon.
- **Teams and logos:** all of them. Avoid the tomahawk chop.
- **Transit and toll brands:** MARTA, CobbLinc, Peach Pass.
- **Media:** radio stations.
- **Agencies:** fictional names and badges, no state seal.
- **"Olympic":** protected by the Ted Stevens Act.
- **People:** real people and their likenesses. Take-Two won *Lohan v. Take-Two*, but only after costly litigation.

### Sensitive sites

The MLK sites, Stone Mountain's carving, the police training center, Oscarville and Lake Lanier, and real crimes.

### City name

**The owner chose to keep real place names** (Atlanta stays Atlanta). If that ever changes, the historical names are "Terminus" (the 1837 railroad name), "Marthasville" (1843) and "Resurgens" (the city motto).
