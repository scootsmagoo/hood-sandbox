# Cast: the protagonist, the crews and the law (Atlanta)

> **Status: draft, 14 Oct 2026. Awaiting the owner's review.** Nothing here gets built until it's approved. Everything here is fictional: no real gangs, people, cases or agencies.

This file belongs to the **Atlanta city pack**. Another city would get its own cast file in the same shape (see [City packs](../README.md#city-packs)).

## The protagonist: Keon "Kudzu" Mabry

**One line:** a laid-off tow-truck driver who knows every lot, back road and impound yard in the city, and has decided to get paid the other way.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 29 |
| **From** | Reynoldstown, raised in his grandmother's house off Memorial Drive |
| **Now** | A weekly-rate room at an extended-stay motel off the Connector (the first safehouse) |
| **Look** | Tall and lean. Locs tied back, a beat-up canvas work jacket (a parody workwear brand), work boots or clean sneakers. The look changes with the wardrobe, which matters for the heat system. |
| **Nickname** | "Kudzu", because you can't get rid of him. Cut him down and he grows back. |

**What he wants, stated plainly:**
1. **Money**, right now. He's behind on the motel.
2. **His grandmother's house.** It was sold after she died, flipped, and is now listed at an absurd price. Buying it back is the sandbox's long-term goal: the most expensive property in the game, and the one that means something.
3. **Not to be anybody's employee again.**

**Why he fits a sandbox with no story:**
- **Tow driving** explains why he can steal cars (hotwiring, repo tricks), why he knows the roads and shortcuts, and why tow, repo and impound jobs are his first side work.
- **Growing up intown and then working all over the metro** lets him walk into every district and crew without being a stranger. He knows people in Zone 6 and he's towed cars out of Buckhead driveways.
- **The house** gives money a purpose without a scripted plot.

**Personality:**
- Dry and quick. He talks to himself in traffic and hates the Top End.
- Polite to old ladies, rude to valets.
- He's not a psychopath, and his lines react to what the player does. Running over pedestrians gets a wince ("…my bad"), not a cheer.
- **He doesn't speak aloud at first.** Like Skyrome, early builds have text barks and grunts, and he gets a voice later.

**Starting state:**
- $300 and a phone.
- The motel room.
- No car. His tow truck was repossessed, which is funny given his job.
- Reputation is **neutral with every crew** except one: [The Ashes](#1-the-ashes) give him a slight plus, because he grew up on their side of town.

**Alternatives,** in case Kudzu isn't right:
- **A Gwinnett kid** who moved intown for a delivery-app job and got deactivated. A suburban outsider who learns the city by driving it.
- **A retired stunt driver** from the film studios, written out of the industry by a production slump.

## The crews

Crews are defined by **turf and trade**, not ethnicity. Members look like the neighborhood they run in, which is mixed almost everywhere in metro Atlanta.

Every crew has:
- **Turf** (the districts they hold)
- **Colors and look** (what their members wear, so the player can read the street)
- **Cars** (what they drive, which also tells the player whose block they're on)
- **Business** (what they make money from, which sets the jobs they offer and the targets they present)
- **A reputation with the player** (−100 to +100):
  - **Positive:** jobs, discounts and backup.
  - **Negative:** they jump you on their turf, and hits on your car.

### 1. The Ashes

- **Who they are:** an east-side crew that has held the same blocks for twenty years and is watching them sell off one flip at a time. They're named after the phoenix on the city seal: they burned down and came back. They're angry at Gulch Holdings and contemptuous of the BeltLine crowd.
- **What they offer Kudzu:** steal-to-order car lists for their chop shop on Moreland (the export-list side activity), and protection on their side of town.
- **What sets them off:** wearing Paces Ferry pastels on their blocks, or stealing from their chop shop.
- **Random events:** a dice game behind a tire shop, a block party with a DJ, a car meet in a closed-down gas station.

### 2. The Paces Ferry Boys

- **Who they are:** trust-fund sons (and a few daughters) of Buckhead money, running a party-drug business out of their parents' pool houses for fun. Untouchable until they're not: their dads' lawyers make city police heat drop fast on their turf, and that cuts both ways.
- **What they offer Kudzu:** "steal my car so I can claim the insurance" jobs, getaway driving after a party goes bad, and delivery runs to Midtown clubs.
- **What sets them off:** touching their cars without being paid to, or embarrassing them in public.
- **Random events:** a pool party you can crash, a valet stand you can rob, a drunk Lambo crash on Peachtree.

### 3. Gulch Holdings

- **Who they are:** a "development company" that is really the biggest crime organization in the city, and the only one with lawyers, lobbyists and police friends. They hire the street crews as muscle and then sell them out.
- **What they offer Kudzu:** well-paid jobs that are dirtier than they look (clearing out a building, making a witness disappear, torching a lot). They also own the listing on his grandmother's house.
- **What sets them off:** interfering with a development site or hitting their parking-lot cash pickups (a robbery side activity).
- **Random events:** a cash pickup from parking-lot kiosks, protesters at a site, a black SUV tailing you after you crossed them.

### 4–6 (built in M3)

- **The Hollowell Royals**: rivals of The Ashes. A Westside crew with deep roots and a business front everyone knows about. They respect Kudzu if he respects their blocks.
- **Night Market**: the takeover and racing crew. They're the gateway to the street-racing side activities, and a good reputation with them unlocks the I-285 night races. They'll race you for pink slips.
- **Kennesaw Iron**: an old-school biker club, at war with Night Market over the roads north of the Perimeter. They offer boat and trailer theft at the lake marinas.

### Crew rules (gameplay)

- **Turf is a layer on the map:** polygons from the city pack. Inside them, crew members hang out at scenario points and crew cars spawn in traffic.
- **Reputation moves with what you do**, not with dialogue:

  | Action | Reputation change |
  |---|---|
  | Help them (jobs) | **Up** with them |
  | Hit their rivals | **Up** with them, **down** with the rivals |
  | Rob, kill or carjack their members | **Down** |
  | Wear their colors | Read as one of them: safe on their turf, a target on their rivals' |

- **Crew wars are random events.** Two crews' members clash at a turf edge. That draws police heat, and it can cover a getaway.
- **Crew violence happens without you.** The police respond to it whether you're there or not. A neighborhood you avoid isn't quiet.

## The law: fictional agencies (Atlanta)

These are parody names that echo the real structure, which matters for the [jurisdiction mechanic](../README.md#crime-heat-and-the-police). No real badges, seals or names.

| In the game | Echoes | Where | Pursuit style |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlanta City Police (ACP)** | APD | Inside the city limits; six patrol zones | Restrictive. No vehicle chase for minor crimes, but camera-heavy ("investigation heat"). |
| **Georgia Highway Patrol (GHP)** | Georgia State Patrol | Interstates, GA-400, the express lanes | Aggressive. PIT at speed, heat escalates fast. |
| **Cobb, Gwinnett, DeKalb and Fulton County Police**, plus city departments (Marietta, Sandy Springs, Doraville…) | The real county and city departments | The suburbs | In between. Small-town departments are ticket-happy. |
| **County sheriffs** | The real sheriffs | Jails, courts, warrants | Serve warrants, transport prisoners. |
| **Transit Authority Police** | MARTA Police | Stations and trains | Control the stations. |
| **Georgia Bureau of Investigation task force** (a fictional name to be decided) | GBI | Heat 4+ | Unmarked cars, helicopters. |
| **Federal task force** | FBI | Heat 5+ | |
| **National Guard** | | Heat 6 | |

County and city names stay real because they're geography. Only the agencies' branding is fictional.
